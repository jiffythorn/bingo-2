Terms & points notice

Plain language, no fine print. What points are (and aren't), how the leaderboard record works, how the bots behave, and the few rules for playing with other people.

Last updated 7 November 2026 · applying to this copy of Bingo 2

Points only — no money anywhere

Bingo 2 uses points. They are a score, not a currency: game pieces, not money.

  • No cash value. Points can't be bought, sold, traded, gifted for money, or cashed out.
  • Nothing goes in or out. No deposits, no withdrawals, no prizes of value, no way to win real money.
  • Nothing is riding on it. Winning or losing points affects your leaderboard standing and nothing else — no one's money, including yours.
  • The prize is standing. Games played, games won and points earned are the reward. That's the whole leaderboard.
  • Not gambling. This isn't a gambling service and isn't connected to one.
  • Bots play too. They're software, they're marked 🤖, and they never collect their winnings.

1. What this site is

Bingo 2 is a free, points-only 75-ball bingo game that runs in your browser. There are no accounts, no ads, no tracking and nothing to install. It is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, and the source is published under the MIT licence — you can read it, fork it, run it offline, or copy it for your own project.

3. Points in Bingo 2 — the economy in plain words

You start with 1,000 points. A table sets what a card costs (Practice is free; the paid tables are 10, 50, 250 and 1,000 points per card), and you can take up to three cards in a round.

  • The pot. Every seat's card cost goes into a shared pot. Six percent is taken as a table fee: half of it feeds the room jackpot and half is simply removed.
  • Payouts. A round runs for a fixed number of balls rather than stopping at the first bingo. The top three finishers are paid 55%, 25% and 12% of the pot; anything left rolls into the jackpot.
  • The jackpot. It starts at ten times the card cost, grows with every card bought, pays out when someone bingos unusually early, and then resets.
  • The record. Every round you're seated for is counted: games played, games won, and how many points you earned. That record is the leaderboard standing — it isn't spendable and can't be exchanged for anything.
  • Saving. Points and the record are kept in this browser's local storage, so reloading keeps both. A restore code (GH2-…) carries the balance and the record to another browser, so they follow you; clearing site data erases them.
  • The host is the bank. In a room, the host's browser keeps the ledger, decides the payout and can be wrong or dishonest. Guests report their own balance when they join, so card costs are an honour system. Play with people you trust.

4. Bot players

Some solo rounds and rooms include computer players. They are always labelled — look for 🤖 or a BOT tag in the lobby and player list.

  • Bots are software, not people. We don't present them as humans and don't use them to inflate a "players online" count.
  • They pay in but never collect. A bot pays its card cost from its own balance like anyone else, but its winnings are discarded — its share goes to the room jackpot. It never takes points out of the pot for itself.
  • You can turn them off. Set bots to None when you create or host a room to play humans only.

5. Rooms, chat and other players

Bingo rooms connect browsers directly to each other — there is no game server in the middle and we don't see your game. The host's browser relays chat between guests.

  • Chat is not moderated in real time and disappears when the room closes. Don't post private information, and don't rely on it being private.
  • Share a room code only with people you trust — anyone with the code can join.
  • We may remove access or report abuse, but we can't watch rooms as they happen. Open an issue for anything serious.

6. Privacy, briefly

  • No accounts, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking pixels.
  • Scores, the name you type, your points and your leaderboard record are kept in your browser's local storage. Clearing site data erases them.
  • Joining a room shares your chosen nickname and point balance with that room. A restore code is a snapshot of your balance and record — anyone who has one can use it, so keep it to yourself.
  • Bingo 2 loads the PeerJS library from a public CDN to connect players. That service sees what any web request sees.
  • Downloaded copies make no requests at all unless you start a room.
